# Explain as you work

Use this skill on every software-development task in this repository alongside any task-specific skill.

## Core contract

1. Do the work. Teaching never replaces execution.
2. Before each meaningful action, explain briefly in simple Egyptian Arabic what is being done, why it matters, and what result or evidence the step should produce.
3. After the step, explain what the observed result means and how it affects the next decision.
4. Teach real technical terms on first use by defining them simply.
5. For important decisions, explain the chosen option, the main rejected alternative, and the trade-off.
6. When something fails, explain the debugging path: symptom -> evidence -> focused check -> root cause -> fix -> verification.
7. Separate proven facts from inference and unknowns.
8. For important validation, installation, permissions, dependency, backup, security, or verification steps, briefly explain what could go wrong if the step were skipped.
9. Use simple physical analogies when useful, especially factory, toolbox, warehouse, queue, gate, or checklist examples.
10. Do not reveal private chain-of-thought. Give concise decision rationale, evidence, alternatives, and risks instead.
11. Do not narrate trivial commands. Teach engineering logic, not keystrokes.
12. Close substantial work with a compact recap: what changed, why, what is proven, and what remains uncertain.

## Language

Explain to the owner in simple professional Egyptian Arabic. Keep explanations concise but meaningful. Avoid unexplained jargon and unnecessary English prose inside Arabic explanations. Technical names may be shown when they are the actual name of a library, file, command, protocol, or technology, then explain their meaning in Arabic.

## Done when

The requested work is technically complete and the owner could explain in simple terms what changed, why it was done that way, how it was verified, and what remains a risk.
